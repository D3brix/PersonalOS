# Knowledge processing

How to turn new information into trustworthy notes in `04_Knowledge/`. Used by [[Inbox_Workflow]].

## 1. Identify what the material is
- **Raw**: unprocessed input (a clipping, export, transcript).
- **Claim**: asserted by someone, not yet checked.
- **Fact**: checked and reliably true.
- **Interpretation**: a reasonable reading of facts.
- **Opinion**: a judgement or preference (the source's, yours, or an AI's).
- **Conclusion**: reasoned from facts; can still be wrong.

One input often mixes several of these. Separate them explicitly in the note.

## 2. Handle uncertainty honestly
- Unknown stays unknown. Do not fill gaps with plausible guesses.
- Label assumptions and unverified claims ("according to ...").
- If the conclusion would flip depending on what is true, stop and ask.

## 3. Verify before calling it knowledge
1. Check it against what is already known.
2. Prefer primary sources over summaries.
3. Check freshness for anything time-sensitive.
4. If it cannot be verified, label it, or leave it in `raw/`.

Match the effort to the stakes.

## 4. Knowledge or raw?
Move material into `04_Knowledge/` only when it is processed, reasonably verified (or visibly labelled), and
likely to be useful later. Never move something just to empty `raw/`.

## 5. Avoid duplicates
Search first (`personalos search`). Extend an existing note instead of creating a parallel one. If new
information contradicts a note, record the contradiction rather than silently overwriting.

## 6. Shape of a good note
- A title that matches how you would search for it.
- A one- or two-sentence summary.
- The substance, with facts, interpretation and opinion visibly separate.
- Open questions and caveats.
- The source, when the information came from somewhere else.
